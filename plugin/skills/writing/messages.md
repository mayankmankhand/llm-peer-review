# Email and chat messages

**Who reads it.** Someone busy, often on a phone, deciding from the first line whether to act now, later or never.

**Order**
1. The request, decision or update, in the first line. An email's subject line says the point.
2. Why, in one or two sentences.
3. What you need from them, by when, and who does what next.

**Rules**
- One ask per message. Two asks become two messages, or a numbered list.
- Fit one phone screen. Move detail to a link or an attachment.
- Keep real politeness, and cut filler ("I hope this finds you well", "just circling back").
- Do not make a sensitive message artificially blunt, and do not add urgency or familiarity the relationship does not have.
- Name dates as dates ("Friday 31 October"), not "end of week".
- A chat message (Slack, LinkedIn) is shorter than an email: no greeting paragraph and no signature block.

**Example.** The release is made up; the ask in the first line is the rule.
- Before: "Hi team, I wanted to give everyone a quick update on where things stand with the release, as there have been a few developments this week."
- After: "The 1.2 release moves to Friday because one test fails on Windows. Can someone with a Windows machine run the tests today and post the result here?"
