# Web page copy and social posts

**Who reads it.** A stranger from a search, a link or a post, usually on a phone, deciding in seconds whether to stay. They ask: am I in the right place, what is this, why should I care, what next?

**Find the reader first.** Before drafting, write a short reader card at the top of the draft, and never publish it: who the page is for (one real person, not "everyone"), where they come from, what they already know, and what they should do next. Ask the owner for these answers, and collect the reader's own words from search terms, support emails or reviews. When nobody knows, label the card a guess and name a way to check it.

**Order**
1. The reader card.
2. The one sentence the page must land.
3. Headline and subhead: what it is, and who it is for.
4. Proof: a real example, story or number, with its source.
5. What they need to act: how it works, the price, common questions.
6. One next step.

**Rules**
- Check every choice against the reader card, and cut what that reader does not need.
- The first phone screen answers "am I in the right place?" in the reader's words, with no inside names.
- Lead with what changes for the reader, not a feature list.
- Put proof beside each claim. Never invent a number, customer or quote.
- Give the page one main action, named by what happens: "Start a free trial", not "Submit".
- In a social post, land the point in the first line, before "see more".
- Test on a fresh reader: ask what the page is for and which line lost them, then fix that line first.

**Example.** The product is made up; what the reader gets, who it is for, and the next step is the rule.
- Before: "We are a passionate team building innovative, AI-powered solutions that help businesses unlock their full potential."
- After: "Turn a month of bank statements into a tax-ready expense report. Built for freelancers who file their own taxes. Try it free with one statement."
